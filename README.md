# CFD-Airfoil-Passive-Flow-Control
CFD investigation of passive flow control using surface dimples on NACA 0018, NACA 0021, and NACA 4412 airfoils in ANSYS Fluent, focusing on lift, drag, flow separation, and aerodynamic performance
## Numerical Investigation of Passive Flow Control on NACA Airfoils

This project presents a numerical investigation of three widely studied airfoils: **NACA 0018, NACA 0021, and NACA 4412**. The study evaluates the aerodynamic performance of these airfoils when **surface dimples are applied as a passive flow-control technique**.

Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations were performed using **ANSYS Fluent** to compare **smooth and dimpled airfoil configurations** at different angles of attack (0°, 5°, 10°, and 15°). The analysis focuses on **lift, drag, flow separation, and overall aerodynamic performance**.

The numerical results demonstrate that the effectiveness of dimples depends on the airfoil geometry and angle of attack. Significant improvements in lift were observed at higher angles of attack, particularly for the **NACA 4412**.

### Key Results

* **NACA 0018:** The dimpled configuration increased lift by approximately **23.90% at 5°**, **10.62% at 10°**, and **26.66% at 15°** compared with the smooth airfoil.

* **NACA 0021:** Lift improvements were observed at higher angles of attack, including approximately **1.89% at 10°** and **18.9% at 15°**.

* **NACA 4412:** Lift increased by approximately **4.00% at 0°**, **6.27% at 5°**, and **19.40% at 10°**.

* The most significant improvement occurred for **NACA 4412 at 15°**, where the dimpled configuration produced approximately **171.57% higher lift** and **45.89% lower drag** than the smooth configuration.

Overall, the results indicate that surface dimples can provide significant aerodynamic benefits under selected operating conditions, particularly near high angles of attack where flow separation becomes increasingly important.

The project includes **mesh development, numerical validation against published literature, aerodynamic coefficient analysis, and flow-field visualization** to investigate the mechanisms responsible for these changes in aerodynamic performance.
